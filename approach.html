Our approach

Why post-mining land.

A depleted mine site comes with cleared ground, road access and a live grid connection — and an owner with a legal obligation to restore it. That combination makes it some of the better ground available for renewable energy, and Mongolia has a great deal of it.

The market

Mongolia’s energy position.

Mongolia’s land is 77% degraded and the country is warming at 2.2°C above pre-industrial levels — close to double the global average.

The policy response is already funded. The government has signed memoranda with EBRD, ACWA Power and UPC covering multi-billion-dollar renewable energy investment. ADB approved a $100 million policy-based loan to catalyse renewables. EBRD invested $218 million in Mongolia in 2025, 53% of it directed to the green economy. The Mongolia Green Finance Corporation launched in August 2026 with $50 million in Green Climate Fund seed capital.

Yet the domestic solar market remains thin. Rural and peri-urban communities, mining operations and industrial facilities lack reliable access to competitively priced equipment and to the technical advisory that green financing requires. That commercial gap is where Urimor Terra starts.

Market drivers

What is changing.

01 / Obligation

$100B+

Closure liabilities

Over one hundred billion dollars in closure provisions sit on mining balance sheets. There are more than 500,000 abandoned mines in the United States, 50,000 in Australia and 10,000 in Canada. No mine in Queensland — the world’s most regulated closure jurisdiction — has ever received a formal closure certificate. The Consolidated Mining Standards Initiative, launching in 2027, replaces industry self-assessment with a single externally governed standard.

02 / Constraint

3M ha

The siting crisis

Solar PV will occupy roughly three million hectares globally by the early 2030s, up from under 500,000 today. Where to site renewables is becoming as difficult a question as whether to build them — land conflict, permitting and grid access now decide which projects reach financial close. Post-mining land arrives with cleared surfaces, road access and existing grid connections already in place.

03 / Capital

$5.5B

Climate finance at scale

Mongolia’s renewable pipeline runs to approximately $5.5 billion over five years, with the institutional stack already assembled: ADB, EBRD, the Green Climate Fund via XacBank, AIIB through Khan Bank, and the newly launched Mongolia Green Finance Corporation. The money exists. What is scarce is bankable, locally originated projects to put it into.

Research

Solar and land restoration together.

Solar development and land restoration are usually treated as competing uses. The research says otherwise.

Peer-reviewed research from Shanghai Jiao Tong University, presented at UNCCD COP17 in Ulaanbaatar in August 2026, shows that photovoltaic arrays on degraded land actively accelerate ecological recovery. At the Kubuqi Desert, gross primary productivity increased by 110 gC/m² and water-use efficiency doubled under the arrays. Since 2017, 92% of new Chinese desert PV plants have adopted ecological construction modes.

COP17 was the first UNCCD conference of parties to host a mining forum. Every major speaker called for integrated post-mine land use planning. The scientific case and the policy case arrived in the same room, in the same week, in the city where this company is based.

Global precedents

  • GCF Sumber Solar, Mongolia — the FP046 facility exceeded all of its targets.
